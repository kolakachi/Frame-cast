# Mascot explainer

A character tells the story. It talks, points, reacts and celebrates while the idea builds around it. The character is the thread that makes it feel like a short film, not a slide deck.

## Look
- The character is a bust, cropped at the chest and bleeding off the bottom edge of the frame like a presenter, at about half the frame height in its hero beats and never under a third in any beat. It sits in its own grid column beside the thing it points at, never parked in a corner with empty space above it.
- In landscape (16:9), a two-column grid: character one side, headline or UI the other, generous margins. In portrait, the character fills the lower half and overlaps the card above it.
- Colour fields flip on key beats: the brand dark, a full accent field for the turn, a light field for the proof, dark again for the close.
- A print texture ties it together: halftone dots or film grain on the character, and flat, clean colour behind it.
- Supporting elements (UI cards, words, props) are simple, graphic and lighter in weight than the character.
- Type: a bold sans for statements, a serif italic for one accent word per line.

## Motion
- The character acts: anticipation before a pose change, a squash or overshoot as it lands, a small idle bob or breathe between beats. It never slides around rigidly.
- It interacts with the frame: points at the card as it lands, leans away in surprise, pushes a word into place, waves at the end.
- Staging: move the character between beats (left, right, close-up, small in a wide shot) so framing changes every beat.
- Cause, then reaction: something happens, then the character reacts; give the reaction a beat.

## Signature move
- One of: a giant-type wipe into the product beat, a stamp on the payoff word, or the window morphing into the product; named in the plan and built with the kit.

## Structure
1. Hook: the character in close-up with the problem, in the first 2 s.
2. Turn: the product or idea appears; the character reacts.
3. Show: 2 to 3 beats where the character points to each feature as it builds.
4. Close: the character celebrates or waves beside the logo and offer.

## Never
- The same pose or the same spot on screen for the whole video.
- A character too small to read, or pushed against the frame edge.
- A copy of someone else's mascot.
