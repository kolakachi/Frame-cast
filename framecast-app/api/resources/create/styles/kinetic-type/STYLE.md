# Kinetic type

The words are the picture. Big type moves with weight and rhythm, one phrase at a time, and the layout itself becomes the motion.

## Look
- Huge display type (Anton, Bebas Neue or a heavy grotesque) that fills 60 to 90% of the frame width.
- Bold flat colour fields; switch the field colour on key beats (at most two field colours plus near-black and near-white).
- One accent word per line: a colour, an outline or a serif italic.
- Small supporting labels in a mono or light sans, well away from the big words.

## Motion
- Type lands with weight: words slam, stretch, slide or stack in, and settle fast. Letters or words stagger by 1 to 3 frames.
- Words transform into the next phrase: a word scales up to become the background, splits apart, or is pushed off by the next line.
- Cuts land on the beat of the music; the field colour flips on the hardest hits.
- Keep energy in holds: a slow scale or drift on the word that stays.

## Structure
1. Hook: one provocative word or question, huge, in frame 1.
2. Build: 4 to 7 short phrases, each its own beat, rising in energy.
3. Turn: one moment of stillness or a single word alone.
4. Close: the name and offer, held.

## Never
- More than 8 words on screen at once.
- A sentence that simply fades in and out.
- Text so fast it cannot be read (see the craft reading-time rule).
