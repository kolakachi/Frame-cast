# Comic book

A story in panels: a problem, the moment it's solved, the happy ending, read like a comic page with the camera
moving from panel to panel. Fun, clear, and great for a feature that fixes a familiar pain.

## Look
- A page of 3-4 panels with thick black borders (10-14 px) and white gutters; the panels hold the story beats in reading order.
- Halftone shading: dot patterns (radial-gradient dots, or WM.grade halftone on pictures) in 2-3 bold flat colours per panel.
- Characters and objects drawn with heavy black outlines (8-10 px) and flat fills; simple faces that act (worried brows, a big smile).
- Lettering: Bangers for captions, sound effects and bubbles; speech bubbles white with a black outline and a tail; sound words in a burst star.

## Motion
- The camera is the storyteller: it moves into each panel (fills the frame), holds while the panel plays, then travels to the next.
- Inside a panel: small acting (a head shake, a bounce), notifications or objects popping (WM.pop), the sound effect stamping on its hit (WM.stamp).
- Captions and bubbles appear as the voice says them, never all at once.
- End on a pull back to the whole page, or on the last panel with the product name in a bubble.

## Structure
1. Panel 1, the problem: the familiar pain, with a caption.
2. Panel 2, the moment: the product does the thing, with a sound effect (CLICK!, WHOOSH!).
3. Panel 3, the result: calm, happy, the feature name in a bubble.
4. Close: the page, the brand and the call to action.

## Never
- Real people's likenesses or known comic characters; the characters are original and simple.
- More than one sound-effect burst per panel.
- Tiny lettering: a caption is readable on a phone at the frame it appears.
