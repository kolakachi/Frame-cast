**Story:** finished ad → product brief → plan and cost → result → specific revision → download → presenter option → next step.

## Production notes

- **One complete example:** save its real inputs, plan, quote, output, revision and download before recording. The opening payoff must come from that example. The proposed coffee end-card copy should fit the selected product.
- **Reference proof:** use a reference whose typography, layout or pacing visibly carries into the result. If the connection is weak, remove the reference sentence and use those seconds for the output.
- **Accurate cost:** show credits, estimates and spending limits as the app displays them. Retain separately priced stages; do not turn an estimate into a fixed cash price in an overlay.
- **Actual checks:** show the real check result. Do not fabricate the assistant message about fixing reading time, or relabel unresolved checks as passed.
- **Bounded edit:** show what actually changed. A spoken-line edit can regenerate a whole talking take, so avoid “Only that part changes” as a universal promise. If this edit runs automatically, show its cost indication instead of inventing an approval screen.
- **Readable screens:** give each shot one thing to read. Keep captions clear of the prompt, cost and Before / After cards. Compress waits with visible time-jump labels.
- **One narrator:** retain the voice-first approach. Record or generate the full read, check its continuity, then cut the picture to it. Use consistent voice settings across beats. During the silent narration window in beat 4, the ad's own audio can play; lower the demo music and avoid overlapping voices.
- **Timing:** the main narration is about 138 words, leaving room to inspect the plan, watch the result and compare the edit. Adjust shot boundaries to the measured narration; do not speed up the voice to preserve provisional timestamps.
- **Pronunciation:** WyvStudio is said “weave studio”: TTS text says “Weave Studio”; captions show WyvStudio.
- **Presenter proof:** beats 1, 7 and 8 are made in the app: Layla's new look saved as a character, her lines from the Sarah read lip-synced by Create's talking-presenter path (VEED Fabric, about $0.15 a second at 720p). So the reveal is true of the performance too. The edit itself is assembled by hand; the video does not claim the whole thing was made inside the app.

## Review of v1

The foundations work: a specific audience, a recognisable problem, one narrator, plan-and-cost approval, a revision moment and a memorable presenter reveal. Keep those. The original has about 190 spoken words over 80.8 seconds, approximately 141 words per minute. The pacing issue is visual density and delayed proof, not excessive word count.

| Gap | Why it matters | Revision |
|---|---|---|
| First product screen at 27.8 seconds. | Roughly a third of the video passes before viewers see how it works. | Real output by second 3; app at second 7. |
| Weekly-algorithm rule, two-week creator delay and “tired by Friday.” | Several broad assertions consume time before the product supplies evidence. | State the no-shoot benefit directly, then prove it. |
| No identifiable product connects the inputs and outputs. | Capabilities are visible, but the viewer's own journey is harder to imagine. | Follow one product through creation, revision and download. |
| “Every line is heard” and “Only that part changes.” | Checks can be unresolved; some edits remake an entire talking take. | Describe the checking process and demonstrate one narrow edit. |
| Beat 5 combines checks, playback and revision; beat 6 adds three workflows. | Too much to read and compare while listening. | Separate result and revision; move composer and connected clients into optional cuts. |
| A reference video is mentioned without showing its effect. | A distinctive benefit feels like another upload option. | Show its visual relationship to the result. |
| No download; one close for both channels. | The workflow stops early and the next step ignores where the viewer is watching. | Complete the download and tailor the close. |
| Presenter animation is produced externally. | Cast availability alone does not prove the app creates that exact performance. | Prefer in-app footage and preserve the manual-assembly distinction. |

**Verification scope:** reviewed against local product positioning, UI and implementation, not a live production run. Exact timing, reference match, presenter availability, credit cost and output quality still depend on the selected capture.

## Presenter: clothing and background

- **Clothing:** solid mid-tone colours; no fine stripes, checks or small prints (they shimmer in generated video); no
  logos or text. Smart-casual, a marketer at work: a soft knit or a relaxed blazer over a plain top. One warm tone that
  sits beside WyvStudio orange without copying it (rust, terracotta, camel). Small earrings only. Identical in every clip.
- **Background:** a real working space with depth: a softly blurred studio or office, shelves, a plant, a warm lamp,
  window light from one side. No screens or readable text behind her, no other people. Neutral and warm, with one orange
  accent object. Clear separation between her and the background. The same place in every clip.
- **Framing for OmniHuman:** a 16:9 image, waist-up, eye level, facing the lens, mouth closed, hands visible and relaxed
  (for gestures), room on one side for captions and the logo.
